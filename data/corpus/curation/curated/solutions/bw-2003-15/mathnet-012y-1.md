With reference to the figure below we have $CR \cdot CP = DQ \cdot DP = CM^2 = DM^2$, which is equivalent to $RC = \frac{DQ \cdot DP}{CP}$. We also have $\frac{AT}{BS} = \frac{AP}{BP} = \frac{AT}{DQ}$, so $AT = \frac{AP \cdot DQ}{BP}$. Since $ABCD$ is cyclic the result now comes from the fact that $DP \cdot BP = AP \cdot CP$ (due to a well-known theorem).
