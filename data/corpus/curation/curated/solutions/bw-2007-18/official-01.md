No. Indeed, if integers $x, y, z, t$ satisfy $x^{2}+y^{2}-3 z^{2}-3 t^{2}=0$, then $x^{2}+y^{2}$ is a multiple of 3 . But squares are congruent to 0 or 1 modulo 3 , so this is possible only if both $x, y$ are divisible by 3 . Then the number $x^{2}+y^{2}=3\left(z^{2}+t^{2}\right)$ is divisible by 9 , the number $z^{2}+t^{2}$ is divisible by 3 , and analogously we see that $z$, $t$ are divisible by 3 . Hence any integer solution to the equation $x^{2}+y^{2}-3 z^{2}-3 t^{2}=0$ has all variables divisible by 3 , and since the equation is homogeneous, this implies $x=y=z=t=0$.
