Solution:

$\angle B A C = 60^{\circ}$, $\angle A B C = 105^{\circ}$ and $\angle A C B = 15^{\circ}$.

Suppose the line $A D$ meets the circumcircle of triangle $A B C$ at $A$ and $E$ (see Figure 5). Let $M$ be the midpoint of $B C$ and $O$ the circumcentre of triangle $A B C$. Since the arcs $B E$ and $E C$ are equal, then the points $O$, $M$, $E$ are collinear and $O E$ is perpendicular to $B C$. From the equality $\angle C D E = \angle A D B = 45^{\circ}$ it follows that $\angle A E O = 45^{\circ}$. Since $|A O| = |E O|$, we have $\angle A O E = 90^{\circ}$ and $A O \parallel D M$.

From the equality $|B D| \cdot |C D| = |A D|^{2}$ we obtain $|A D| = |D E|$, which implies that $|O M| = |M E|$. Therefore $|B O| = |B E|$ and also $|B O| = |E O|$. Hence the triangle $B O E$ is equilateral. This gives $\angle B A E = 30^{\circ}$, so $\angle B A C = 60^{\circ}$. Summing up the angles of the triangle $A B D$ we obtain $\angle A B C = 105^{\circ}$ and from this $\angle A C B = 15^{\circ}$.
