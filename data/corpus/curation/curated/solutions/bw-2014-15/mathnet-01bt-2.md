Consider an inscribed quadrilateral $A'B'C'D'$ with sides of the same lengths as $ABCD$ (it exists because we can draw these $4$ sides in a big circle and after that continuously decrease its radius). Then $\angle B' + \angle D' = 180^\circ < \angle B + \angle D$, therefore $\angle B' < \angle B$ or $\angle D' < \angle D$ and hence $A'C' < AC$ by cosine theorem.

So the inequality under consideration for $A'B'C'D'$ is stronger than that for $ABCD$. But the inequality for $A'B'C'D'$ immediately follows from Ptolemy's theorem. ◀
