Let $s$ be a circumcircle $ABD$. Then the point $C$ is outside this circle but inside the angle $BAD$.

Apply the inversion with the center $A$ and radius $1$. This inversion maps the circle $s$ to the line $s' = B'D'$, where $B'$ and $D'$ are images of $B$ and $D$. The point $C$ goes to the point $C'$ inside the triangle $AB'D'$. Therefore $B'C' + C'D' < AB' + AC'$.

Now, due to inversion properties we have $B'C' = \frac{BC}{AB} \cdot AC$, $C'D' = \frac{CD}{AC} \cdot AD$, $AB' = \frac{1}{AB}$, $AC' = \frac{1}{AC}$. And so $\frac{BC}{AB} \cdot AC + \frac{CD}{AC} \cdot AD < \frac{1}{AB} + \frac{1}{AC}$. Multiplying by $AB \cdot AC \cdot AD$ we obtain a desirable inequality. ◀
