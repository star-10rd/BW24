Solution:
Assume $a_{i} = k + d i$ for $i = 1, 2, \ldots, n$. Then $k$ is a multiple of every $i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n-1\}$ but not a multiple of $n$. If $n = a b$ with $a, b > 1$ and $\gcd(a, b) = 1$, then $k$ is divisible by both $a$ and $b$, but not by $n$, which is a contradiction. Hence, $n$ has only one prime factor.
