Let $M$ be the third circle, and denote by $Z$ the point on $M$ diametrically opposite to $P$.
Since $\angle AX P = \angle PX Z = 90^\circ$, the three points $A$, $X$, $Z$ are collinear. Likewise, the three points $B$, $Y$, $Z$ are collinear. Point $P$ is equidistant to the three vertices of triangle $ABZ$, for $PA = PB = PZ$ is the common diameter of the circles. Therefore $P$ is the circumcentre of $ABZ$, which means the perpendiculars $PX$ and $PY$ bisect the sides $AZ$ and $BZ$. Ergo, $X$ and $Y$ are midpoints on $AZ$ and $BZ$, which leads to the desired conclusion $XY \parallel AB$.
