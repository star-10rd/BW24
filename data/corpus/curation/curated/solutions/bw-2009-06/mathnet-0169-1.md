It is sufficient to prove for each prime $p$ that if $b$ is divisible by $p^{2k-1}$ but not divisible by $p^k$ for some positive integer $k$, then $p$ divides $a$.
Let $u$, $v$, $w$ be the integer roots of the equation. Then by Viète's formulas $u + v + w = a$, $uv + uw + vw = 0$, $uvw = b$. Let $u$ be divisible by $p$. It follows from the equality $vw = -u(v + w)$ that $v$ or $w$ is divisible by $p$. Let $v$ be divisible by $p$. If $w$ is not divisible by $p$ then $u$ and $v$ are divisible by the same power of $p$ and hence if $b$ is divisible by $p^{(2k-1)}$ for some positive integer $k$, it also has to be divisible by $p^{(2k)}$, contradicting our initial assumptions about $p$. Hence, $w$ is also divisible by $p$ and $a = u + v + w$ is divisible by $p$, too.
