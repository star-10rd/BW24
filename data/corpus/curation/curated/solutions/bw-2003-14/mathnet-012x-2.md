Solution 2:

Let us denote the midpoints of the segments $MN$, $NK$, $KM$ by $B_{1}$, $C_{1}$, $A_{1}$, respectively. It is easy to see that triangle $A_{1}B_{1}C_{1}$ is homothetic to triangle $NKM$ via the homothety centered at the intersection of the medians of triangle $NMK$ and dilation $-\frac{1}{2}$. The perpendiculars through $M$, $N$, $K$ to $AB$, $BC$, $CA$, respectively, are also the perpendicular bisectors of these sides, so they intersect in the circumcentre of triangle $ABC$. The desired result follows now from the homothety, and we find that the common point of intersection is the circumcentre of the image of triangle $ABC$ under the homothety; that is, the circumcentre of the triangle with vertices the midpoints of the sides $AB$, $BC$, $CA$.
