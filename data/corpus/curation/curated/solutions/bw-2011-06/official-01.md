Let $n^{\prime}=[n / 2]$ be the largest integer satisfying $n^{\prime} \leq n / 2$. We solve the problem with $n^{2}-3 n^{\prime 2}$ instead of $n^{2} / 4$, which is exactly what we are supposed to do if $n$ is even and slighly better than our original goal if $n$ is odd.

A point is called relevant if both of its coordinates are integers between 1 and $n$, inclusively. A relevant point is called tiny if both of its coordinates are at most $n^{\prime}$ and large otherwise. Note that there are $n^{\prime 2}$ tiny points. A line through the origin is called vicious if contains at least two relevant points.

Consider any vicious line $\ell$. Suppose that $\ell$ passes through exactly $k$ vicious points and that $P=(x, y)$ is that one among them that is closest to the origin. Defining $P_{i}=(x \cdot i, y \cdot i)$ for all integers $i$, the relevant points on $\ell$ are $P_{1}, P_{2}, \ldots, P_{k}$. Since $k \geq 2$, it follows that $P=P_{1}$ itself is tiny. Now let $k^{\prime}$ denote that positive integer for which $P_{1}, P_{2}, \ldots, P_{k^{\prime}}$ are the tiny points on $\ell$, whereas $P_{k^{\prime}+1}, \ldots, P_{k}$ are the large points on $\ell$. Since $P_{k^{\prime}+1}$ is not tiny, the point $P_{2\left(k^{\prime}+1\right)}$ cannot be relevant, for which reason $k \leq 2 k^{\prime}+1$; in particular, it follows that $k \leq 3 k^{\prime}$.

Summing the inequality just obtained over all vicious lines, we learn that the number of all relevant points lying on vicious lines is at most three times the number of all tiny points, i.e. at most $3 n^{\prime 2}$. Thus there are at least $n^{2}-3 n^{\prime 2}$ relevant points not belonging to any vicious line. As expained in the beginning, this solves the problem.
