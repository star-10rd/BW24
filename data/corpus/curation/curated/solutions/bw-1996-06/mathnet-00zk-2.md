Solution:
Let $r = \operatorname{gcd}(a, c)$ and $s = \operatorname{gcd}(b, d)$. Let $a = a' r$, $b = b' s$, $c = c' r$ and $d = d' s$. Then $a' b' = c' d'$. But $\operatorname{gcd}(a', c') = 1$ and $\operatorname{gcd}(b', d') = 1$, so we must have $a' = d'$ and $b' = c'$. This gives
$$
a + b + c + d = a' r + b' s + c' r + d' s = a' r + b' s + b' r + a' s = (a' + b')(r + s).
$$
Since $a'$, $b'$, $r$ and $s$ are positive integers, $a + b + c + d$ is not a prime.
