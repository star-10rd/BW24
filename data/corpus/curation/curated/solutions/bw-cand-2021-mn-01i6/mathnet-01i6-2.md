**Solution 2.** Let $\omega$ be the circle with center $K$ an radius $KD$, as in figure 19. Then $\omega$ is tangent to $AD$ and $AL$. Let $BC$ intersect $\omega$ at $D$ and $Q$. Let $BM$ intersect $\omega$ at $L$ and $R$. Let $QP$ intersect $BL$ at $S$.
Cross-ratio chasing gives, through the projections $BL \to D$-pencil $\to \omega \to L$-pencil $\to BC \to P$-pencil $\to BL$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(L, R; M, B) &= (DL, DR; DM, DB) = (L, R; D, Q) = (LC, LB; LD, LQ) \\
&= (C, B; D, Q) = (PC, PB; PD, PQ) = (M, B; L, S) = (L, S; M, B),
\end{aligned}
$$
therefore $R = S$.
It is clear now that $P$ lies on the polar lines of both $A$ and $B$ with respect to $\omega$, therefore $AB$ is the polar line of $P$. This implies that $PK \perp AB$.
