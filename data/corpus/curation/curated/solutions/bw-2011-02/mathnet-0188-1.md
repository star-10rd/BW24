First, setting $y = f(x)$ one obtains $f(0) = 0$. Secondly $y = 0$ yields $f(f(x)) = 0$ for all $x$, thus
$$
f(f(x) - y) = f(y).
$$
Setting $x = 0$ yields $f(-y) = f(y)$, and finally $y := -z$ yields
$$
f(f(x) + z) = f(-z) = f(z).
$$
If $f(x) = 0$ for all $x$, then $f$ is obviously bounded. If on the other hand there exists an $x_0$ such that $f(x_0) \neq 0$, then, with $x = x_0$, the last equality gives that $f$ is periodic with period $|f(x_0)|$ and thus $f$ must be bounded.
