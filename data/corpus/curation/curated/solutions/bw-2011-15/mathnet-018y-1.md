Let $F$ be the point symmetric to $E$ with respect to the line $DB$. Then the equality $\angle ADB = \angle BDC$ shows that $F$ lies on the line $DC$, on the same side of $D$ as $C$. Moreover, we have $AE \cdot ED < CD \cdot AE$, or $FD = ED < CD$, so in fact $F$ lies on the segment $DC$.

![](attached_image_1.png)

Note now that triangles $DEB$ and $DFB$ are congruent (symmetric with respect to the line $DB$), so $\angle AEB = \angle BFC$. Also, we have
$$
BE^2 = CD \cdot AE - AE \cdot ED = AE \cdot (CD - ED) = AE \cdot (CD - FD) = AE \cdot CF.
$$
Therefore
$$
\frac{BE}{AE} = \frac{CF}{BE} = \frac{CF}{BF}.
$$
This shows that the triangles $BEA$ and $CFB$ are similar, which gives $\angle EBA = \angle FCB = \angle DCB$, as desired.
