From similar right triangles we get
$$
\frac{BE}{AE} = \frac{\frac{BD}{AD}ED}{\frac{AD}{BD}ED} = \left(\frac{BD}{AD}\right)^2
$$
and analogously
$$
\frac{CF}{AF} = \left(\frac{CD}{AD}\right)^2.
$$
Now, because $AC > AB$, the lines $BC$ and $EF$ intersect in a point $X$ on the extension of segment $BC$ beyond $B$. Menelaos' theorem gives
$$
BX \frac{CF}{AF} = CX \frac{BE}{AE}, \quad CX \frac{DG}{AG} = DX \frac{CF}{AF}, \quad DX \frac{BE}{AE} = BX \frac{DG}{AG}.
$$
Adding these relations and rearranging terms we arrive at
$$
BC \frac{DG}{AG} = BD \frac{CF}{AF} + CD \frac{BE}{AE} = BC \frac{BD \cdot CD}{AD^2} = BC \frac{AD \cdot HD}{AD^2} = BC \frac{HD}{AD},
$$
whence
$$
\frac{DG}{AG} = \frac{HD}{AD}.
$$
